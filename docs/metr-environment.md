# METR Environment

<div class="draft4-watermark" aria-hidden="true"></div>

This section describes the environments in which METR operates — regulatory, roadway, distribution, communications, and regional — and why trustworthiness is central to making **[electronic regulations](https://isotc204.org/iso24315-vocab/latest/terms/electronic%20regulation/)** usable at scale.

METR is designed to operate within the following environmental constraints:

- **[Regulatory environment](regulatory-environment.md):** Electronic rules and regulations already exist in law, created by authorized actors inside overlapping governments. This page sets out the constraints on what can be published, by whom, for where, and when it is in force, including the difference between approval, implementation, and activation.
- **[Transport environment](transport-environment.md):** Most vehicle miles are on familiar routes, but the operator must still comply when a trip is new or changes with little notice. The page explains the resulting limits on storage, wireless connectivity, and privacy, and why METR lets the vehicle choose which rules to request.
- **[Roadway environment](roadway-environment.md):** Signs, markings, signals, and other traffic control devices remain how many regulations are posted and enforced. This page covers how electronic regulations are different from physical traffic control devices, and how field feedback keeps the two consistent.
- **[Communications environment](communications-environment.md):** Distribution systems have to reach consumer systems over connections that fail and that differ by path, from a centre, the roadside, or the vehicle. Emergent updates still have to arrive in time to be used.
- **[Regional environment](regional-environment.md):** Each region adopts its own guidelines so travel inside the region can stay interoperable without forcing every region to use the same protocols, radios, or credentials. This page describes the three-layer model for implementation and what a vehicle needs in order to cross from one region to another.
- **[Need for trustworthiness](need-for-trustworthiness.md):** A user has to be able to determine that a rule is legitimate, correctly represented, current, and applicable to the location, time, and road user. This page discusses how the various elements of trustworthiness that are addressed by the METR solution.
- **[Functional view](functional-view.md):** This page provides an overview of the functional view of the METR reference architecture as defined in ISO/TR 24315-2. It explains hpw a legal regulation is translated, checked, approved, and distributed, and how a transport user applies what is relevant. It also covers reporting and resolving discrepancies between electronic records and the roadway.
