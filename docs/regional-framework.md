==### Purpose of Page==

==This page is intended to serve as a template for regional governments to develop their own implementation policies for the deployment and operation of Management of Electronic Traffic Regulations (METR) system of systems. It provides a structured approach to creating a policy framework that can be customized to meet the specific needs and contexts of different regions. As a prerequisite for establishing METR deployments, this page outlines the essential steps and considerations that regional authorities must address to ensure successful implementation, operation, and optimization of METR.==

==### Audience==

==This guide is designed for regional government officials, transportation planners, policy makers, and other stakeholders involved in the deployment and management of transportation regulations. It is also a valuable resource for IT and infrastructure professionals tasked with implementing and maintaining METR systems. Additionally, this page may be of interest to legal experts and consultants advising on regulatory compliance and system integration.==

### Need for Regional Implementation Guidance

As METR systems become more widespread, regional implementation guides are essential to ensure interoperability across different jurisdictions, as explained in the [regional body perspective](regional-body-perspective.md). These guides provide a consistent framework that aligns with national and international standards while allowing for local customization. Without clear regional guidance, there is a risk of fragmented or incompatible systems that could undermine the effectiveness of METR, leading to potential safety issues, inefficiencies, and challenges in regulation enforcement. This page provides a **draft annotated outline of a regional implementation guide**. Portions of the guide can be delegated to lower levels (e.g., to a national or sub-national bodies) as appropriate to each region.

## Primary Needs for Regional METR Deployment

The regional implementation guide should provide an analysis of the needs of the region, including a comprehensive needs assessment. In particular, this analysis should consider the level of communication connectivity across the region, the current state of digitized R&Rs within the region, the level of support for automated driving within the region, the expected breadth and depth of METR regulations to be deployed within the region, and the expected trends in each of these areas.

## Expected Regional METR Governance Structure

The regional implementation guide should provide a description of the expected governance structure for the region, including the roles and responsibilities of the various stakeholders involved in the deployment and operation of METR systems. In particular, this description should consider the following:

- The roles and responsibilities of the regional body
- The roles and responsibilities of the regulation systems
- The roles and responsibilities of the distribution systems
- The roles and responsibilities of the discrepancy handling systems
- The roles and responsibilities of the service discovery systems
- The expected stakeholders for each of the above systems

## Expected Regional METR Budget Responsibilities

The regional implementation guide should provide a description of the expected budget responsibilities for the region, including the expected costs to be borne by the various stakeholders involved in the deployment and operation of METR systems. In particular, this description should consider the following:

- The expected costs to be borne by the regional body
- The expected costs to be borne by the rule makers
- The expected costs to be borne by the infrastructure owners/operators
- The expected costs to be borne by the METR component system owners/operators
- The expected costs to be borne by the fleet managers, navigators, and other planners
- The expected costs to be borne by the vehicle operators
- The expected costs to be borne by the ADS developers and map providers
- The expected costs to be borne by the enforcement and emergency agencies

## Expected Regional METR Legal and Policy Framework Responsibilities

The regional implementation guide should indicate the legal and policy framework that will be used to support the deployment of METR systems across the region. For example, the guide should indicate the following:

- the expected monitoring and evaluation strategies for METR systems,
- the expected credentialing requirements for METR systems, and
- the expected reporting requirements for METR systems.

## Expected Regional METR Stakeholder Engagement Responsibilities

The regional implementation guide should indicate the stakeholders that will be engaged in the deployment and operation of METR systems across the region. For example, the guide should indicate the following:

- the expected stakeholders for the regional body,
- the expected stakeholders for the rule makers,
- the expected stakeholders for the infrastructure owners/operators,
- the expected stakeholders for the METR component system owners/operators,
- the expected stakeholders for the fleet managers, navigators, and other planners,
- the expected stakeholders for the vehicle operators,
- the expected stakeholders for the ADS developers and map providers,
- the expected stakeholders for the enforcement and emergency agencies,
- the expected stakeholders for the public and private sector,
- the expected stakeholders for the research and development community,

## Expected Regional METR Timeline

The regional implementation guide should indicate expectations for the timeline to deploy METR systems across the region. For example, the guide should indicate the expected timeline for the deployment of: 

- regional policies and regulations,
- cybersecurity credentialing systems,
- regulation systems, 
- various regulation types and how widely they will be available,
- infrastructure necessary to distribute emergent regulations,
- fully automated vehicles, 
- etc. 

## Regional Reference Architecture

A reference architecture serves as a blueprint for the deployment of METR systems, offering a tailored version of the ISO reference architecture that aligns with the specific needs of a region. The regional reference architecture specifies preferred deployment scenarios, such as determining which entity will serve as the distribution system operator. This choice is critical as it impacts the efficiency, security, and reliability of the METR system.

Different options for the distribution system operator include government agencies, private operators, or public-private partnerships, each with different trade-offs. For instance, a government-operated distribution system may offer greater control and security, but could be slower to adapt to technological changes. Conversely, a private operator might bring innovation and efficiency but could raise concerns about accountability and public trust. Regional authorities must weigh these options carefully, considering factors such as cost, scalability, and regulatory compliance.

The regional reference architecture should also indicate how distribution systems can identify and obtain all regulations relevant to their system. For example, is there a single collection system for the region or how does a distribution system identify and connect to existing and new regulation systems?

### Policies for Regulation Verification

Independent verification of regulations within the METR system offers significant benefits, including increased trust in the system, enhanced safety, and compliance with legal standards. However, it also presents challenges, such as the need for robust verification protocols, potential delays in regulation implementation, and additional costs. Establishing clear policies for regulation verification is crucial to balance these benefits and challenges, ensuring that regulations are accurate, reliable, and effectively enforced while minimizing disruptions to the system.

### Policies for Deployment

Regional policies related to METR deployment must address the trade-offs between centralized and decentralized approaches. For example, establishing an Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) directive that mandates the availability of certain categories of METR information across all regional agencies can promote uniformity and interoperability. However, it may limit the flexibility of local governments to tailor the system to their specific needs.

Alternatively, allowing local governments to decide independently whether to deploy METR information and which regulation categories to support provides greater customization and responsiveness to local conditions but could lead to inconsistencies and gaps in coverage. Regional authorities must carefully consider these trade-offs, aiming to create a policy framework that balances standardization with local autonomy, ensuring that METR deployment is both effective and adaptable to changing needs.
